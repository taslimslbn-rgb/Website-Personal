Meeting 1 · Material

Generic Structure of Narrative Text

A narrative text is built from five stages that carry the story from beginning to end.

Orientation

Introduces the characters, setting (time & place), and background of the story.

Complication

A problem or conflict arises that disturbs the characters' situation.

Falling Action

The characters respond and take steps that begin to move toward solving the problem.

Resolution

The conflict is solved, for better or worse — the tension is released.

Evaluation / Coda

A closing comment, lesson, or moral message the story leaves the reader with.

Tip

Not every narrative labels these parts explicitly — look for shifts in tension and time connectives as clues to where each stage begins.

Meeting 1 · Applied Example

"The Mysterious Old House" — Structure in Action

Orientation
Once upon a time, in a small village near the forest, there lived a curious girl named Rani. She loved exploring old, abandoned places whenever she had free time.
Complication
One afternoon, Rani suddenly noticed an old house she had never seen before. She pushed open the creaking door and stepped inside. Suddenly, the door slammed shut, and she heard strange whispers from upstairs.
Falling Action
Her heart pounded, but she took a deep breath and slowly climbed the stairs. After that, she found an old music box playing by itself, and realized the whispers were only wind through a broken window.
Resolution
Then, Rani closed the window latch, and the whispering stopped immediately. The door opened smoothly this time, and she walked outside safely.
Evaluation / Coda
Finally, Rani realized that not everything mysterious is actually dangerous. From that day on, she became braver and more curious about solving mysteries instead of fearing them.

Simple Past Tense

Form: Subject + V2 (regular: -ed / irregular form)

Use: A completed action at a specific point in the past.

Example: Rani walked into the old house.

Past Continuous Tense

Form: Subject + was/were + V-ing

Use: An action in progress at a specific past moment, often interrupted by another action.

Example: She was climbing the stairs when she heard a noise.

PatternExampleMeaning
Simple Past + Simple PastShe opened the door and screamed.Two completed actions, one after another.
Past Continuous + Simple PastWhile she was climbing, she heard a noise.An ongoing action interrupted by a shorter one.

Direct Speech vs Indirect Speech

🔒

Meeting 3 Theory — Password Required

Ask your teacher for the password to view this material.

Incorrect password. Try again.

Direct speech quotes the exact words a character says. Indirect (reported) speech reports what was said, usually with a tense backshift.

Direct SpeechIndirect SpeechRule
"I am scared," Rani said.Rani said (that) she was scared.am/is → was
"I will open the window," she said.She said (that) she would open the window.will → would
"I have lived here," he said.He said (that) he had lived there.have + V3 → had + V3
"Where does the noise come from?" she asked.She asked where the noise came from.question word order, no auxiliary
"Close the door!" the voice said.The voice told her to close the door.imperative → to + V1

Time Sequence Connectives

🔒

Meeting 3 Theory — Password Required

Ask your teacher for the password to view this material.

Incorrect password. Try again.

Connectives link events in the order they happen, guiding the reader smoothly through the plot.

then

Marks the next event in a simple sequence.
Then, Rani closed the window latch.

suddenly

Marks an unexpected, quick event.
Suddenly, the door slammed shut.

after that

Marks an event happening once the previous one finishes.
After that, she found a music box.

finally

Marks the last event or the story's conclusion.
Finally, Rani realized the truth.
